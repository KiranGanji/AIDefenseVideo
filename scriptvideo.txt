
The threat is no longer isolated. It is structural. Providers and revenue cycle partners are using AI to reverse engineer payer policies and optimize coding at scale - driving higher medical cost and lower affordability.

AI Defense is our response: a modular capability suite built across Policy and Benefit, Claims, Network, and Clinical Intelligences, along with state of art Agentic Orchestration — all connected through a modular architecture designed for secure, auditable execution.

Across the portfolio, we have already identified over 20 million dollars in combined exposure opportunity — and that number is growing as each module matures.

Impact Radar benchmarks providers against true peer cohorts to detect abnormal coding and utilization shifts.
We identified nine billing entities whose ER-to-inpatient conversion rates were three-and-a-half to six times higher than comparable peers. Most inpatient stays were only one to three days, yet one provider had ninety-four percent of allowed spend in inpatient services. Across sixty-eight thousand claims, that is roughly two-point-one million dollars in exposure. A small set of providers systematically converting ER visits into inpatient admissions at rates patient severity alone cannot explain.

Adversarial Claim Simulator thinks like a revenue optimizer — simulating reimbursement-maximizing tactics before they scale.
It uncovered five laboratory providers using Modifier 59 and X-series modifiers at rates between thirty-five and eighty-nine percent of claim lines — versus peer medians under five percent. All five exceeded the ninety-ninth percentile. The pattern was consistent: base lab codes paired with modifier-separated add-on lines, same date, same patient, same encounter. This is not random variation — it is systematic unbundling designed to maximize reimbursement. Estimated exposure: one-point-eight million dollars across forty-three thousand claim lines.

Retro AI Editing turns reimbursement policy into machine-executable logic and exposes where payments diverge from policy intent.
It found consultation CPT codes still being submitted and paid despite a non-reimbursement policy effective since October 2019. The policy is absolute — these codes should not be reimbursed regardless of modifier or specialty. Yet in 2025, nearly four thousand claim lines flowed through, representing over one-point-one million in recovery potential. Claims passed automation but consistently violated policy intent — exactly the kind of drift AI-assisted billing optimization exploits, and exactly what this module catches.

Third Party Graph connects providers, CPT patterns, geography, and clinical context — turning isolated anomalies into actionable intelligence.
It flagged twenty-two providers across seventeen states whose spinal injection utilization far exceeded benchmarks. Their members averaged nearly seven injections per year — three times the national norm. The pattern spans pain medicine, anesthesiology, and neurology, with repeated multi-line procedures on the same members over time. Claims were largely paid with minimal denials — this is not a billing-error signal, it is a utilization-abuse signal. Estimated exposure: one-point-one million dollars.
